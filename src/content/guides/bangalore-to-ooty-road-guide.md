Ooty is the queen of South Indian hill stations, and the drive there is half the trip — eucalyptus air, a tiger-reserve crossing where spotted deer graze by the tarmac, and one of India's most famous hairpin climbs. It's also a drive with a hard rule that catches first-timers: **the forest closes at night**.

## The route, in three acts

**Act one: Bangalore → Mysore → Gundlupet (about 4 hours).** The Mysore expressway does the heavy lifting; past Mysore you're on the smooth Nanjangud–Gundlupet stretch. Breakfast before Mysore, fuel at Gundlupet.

**Act two: the Bandipur crossing.** From Gundlupet the highway runs straight through **Bandipur Tiger Reserve** — speed limits are enforced, stopping is prohibited, and sightings of deer, elephants and gaur from the car are genuinely common. The non-negotiable: **the gate closes 9 PM to 6 AM**. Every good Ooty plan is built around crossing in daylight, both ways.

**Act three: the climb.** Two choices from the forest side:

| Route | Character | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Kalhatti (via Masinagudi) | 36 hairpins, steep, dramatic | Shorter; confident stomachs |
| Via Gudalur | Longer, gentler gradient | Kids, elders, full vehicles |

On either climb the AC goes off for the steepest sections — standard ghat practice. Prone to motion sickness? Front seat, light breakfast, and take Gudalur.

## Where to stop

- **Gundlupet's sunflower fields** (seasonal, post-monsoon) — the classic photo stop.
- **Bandipur reception zone** — safari bookings if you're overnighting at Masinagudi.
- **Pykara Lake and the Wenlock Downs "9th Mile" shooting spot** — on the Ooty side, better saved for the return or a dedicated morning.

## The honest plan

Leave Bangalore at 5:30–6 AM, cross Bandipur by early afternoon, and check in with the whole evening for the Botanical Gardens or the lake. Do Doddabetta, the tea museum and Coonoor the next morning, and start back by 2 PM to make the forest gate comfortably.
