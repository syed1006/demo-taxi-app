The Bangalore–Mysore run changed completely when the access-controlled expressway (NH-275) opened: what used to be a horn-heavy three-hour grind is now a smooth 90-minute cruise. But the old Mysore Road didn't stop being worth driving — it just became the scenic option. Here's how to choose, and what it costs.

## The expressway: fast and boring (in a good way)

The 119 km expressway runs from the Bangalore fringe (entry near Kengeri / NICE Road junction) to the Mysore Ring Road. It is fully access-controlled — no tractors, no bikes, no random U-turns — with a 100 km/h limit for cars. City-to-city, plan on **90 minutes to 2 hours** depending on where in Bangalore you start; getting from central Bangalore to the expressway entry can take as long as the expressway itself on a weekday morning.

Two things to know. First, **tolls**: there are toll plazas along the route, and a car pays roughly **₹150–175 each way** (rates are revised periodically — the driver's FASTag receipt shows the exact figure). Second, **facilities are sparse**: fuel up and take the restroom break before you enter, because exits are limited by design.

## The old road: stops worth slowing down for

State Highway 17 — the classic Mysore Road through Ramanagara, Channapatna, Maddur and Srirangapatna — is slower but full of reasons to stop:

- **Ramanagara** — the granite hills from *Sholay*; silk-cocoon market town.
- **Channapatna** — GI-tagged wooden toys; the lacquerware shops line the highway.
- **Maddur** — the legendary **Maddur vada** with filter coffee; the tiffin rooms near the bus stand are the real deal.
- **Srirangapatna** — Tipu Sultan's island fort, 15 minutes before Mysore, worth a half-day by itself.

## The smart cab strategy

Our drivers' default for day trips: **expressway out, old road back**. You reach Mysore fresh by mid-morning, do the palace and Chamundi Hills at leisure, then unwind on the return with a Maddur coffee stop. On the [Mysore 1-Day Tour Package](/tour-packages/mysore-1-day-tour/) this routing is built in, with tolls and parking already in the price.

Avoid leaving Mysore between 5 and 7 PM on Sundays — half of Bangalore has the same idea. Leaving after the Brindavan Gardens fountain show (around 8 PM) actually gets you home faster.
